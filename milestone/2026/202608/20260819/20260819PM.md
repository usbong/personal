According to Okamoto-san, while Nintendo's sales have gone down by 9.5% to 517.8 billion yen in comparison with its last year's sales in the same period, the studio's earnings have risen by 150.5% to as much as 142.6 billion yen.

In addition, while the price of Nintendo's shares appears to cost 7,000 yen, the truth is that it's now gone up to at least 8,000 yen. Okamoto-san further explains in a chart that this figure is beginning to rise again after its fall in September last year.

Okamoto-san also recounts that one reason that people say why the value of Nintendo's shares is rising again is that the studio is showing impressive earnings. Okamoto-san also points out that the software sales for Switch1 and Switch2 were also very impressive. Specifically, Okamoto-san gives the example of the game, "Tomodachi Collection," a title that he himself plays and has sold 8 million globally.

However, Okamoto-san thinks that this achievement doesn't immediately mean that the value of Nintendo's shares is going to continue to further rise from hereon, because, as Okamoto-san explains, the movement in the value of shares is influenced by people's predictions or forecasts. In particular, the Switch2's price is foreseen to go up subsequently. Moreover, there's another concern that the cost of the memory into which downloaded content is stored would increase. Therefore, although Nintendo could sell the Switch2, it's believed that the studio's earnings won't be able to go up as much.

Further still, given that the stock market is also moved by people's emotions, it appears that there are other shares in the stock market that are better than the ones from Nintendo such as those related to AI.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that the point that investors are concerned about the most is whether or not Nintendo will be able to make more money from its hardware, that is, the Switch2. Although Okamoto-san is still not buying more shares in addition to what he currently own for the reasons that he's stated, he looks forward to the dividends that he would receive this September, and he encourages all Nintendo fans, who might want to buy shares, to do so for the long term, instead of the short.
