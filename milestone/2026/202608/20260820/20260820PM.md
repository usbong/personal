Okamoto-san recounts that during the war, Levi's designed pants that had the letter "M" at the back. However, their client told them that this design wasn't necessary, hence, there remained some pants that had already been made and had this letter. Furthermore, there were people who thought that it was better to paint the stitches of this letter. This became an example of the style of that era, although the jeans that had this paint have already lost it after half a century.

Okamoto-san next reiterates that he doesn't wash his jeans more than once per year, because he prefers to preserve the color through this method. In addition, he reveals that he has four pants that he combines with belts that have distinct colors for him to be able to distinguish them well, and he washes all of them together. Thus, he thinks that women don't wear jeans, and for those, who do wear them, they wash them much more than once every year.

Okamoto-san also teaches us that Levi's jeans have marks such as 501 or 301 that signify the decade when they were released. Therefore, the 501 jeans were released in the 50s. As Okamoto-san says, these pants cost millions of yen, a price so expensive that not everyone, even him, could buy.

However, he recounts that when he was working at Capcom, he was able to buy some of the pants of that era such as Mr. YAMANE's "Evisu," which began as a tribute to Levi's, without the letter, "L," and which his co-workers could also buy and wear.

Finally, Okamoto-san answers the questions of his viewers. For example, he shares that while he sleeps very shortly, for only about three hours, it's a habit that he needed to develop when he was working at Capcom. However, he admits that when his head would hurt, he sleeps for an hour. Moreover, he teaches that more than losing money by getting scammed or some other deception, the worst thing to happen is to lose our memories, because, in the final analysis, we live to create memories after all.

This is all. As always, there's much more to say, but I should already stop here.
