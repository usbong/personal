First, Okamoto-san clarifies that he's not related to Ms. Kikuchi at all, but since he lives in Malaysia, he wants to share his thoughts about this topic.

Okamoto-san says that it's a very understandable decision, given that Japan's situation seems to be not yet improving right away, although he warns that it isn't easy to leave and return to Japan continuously.

Furthermore, he thinks that Ms. Kikuchi and her husband want to give their daughters the best opportunity possible today in order to secure their futures. Specifically, Okamoto-san points out that the international schools in Malaysia teach in the English language, especially British English, so their eldest daugther, who would be starting her first year in primary school, will be able to become bilingual. Moreover, given that Malaysia has a culture that allows the hiring of domestic helpers, when Ms. Kikuchi, who works as a Japanese language teacher in Malaysia, returns to Japan, she won't need to worry about her daughters. Both countries, Malaysia and Japan, are very strict in terms of rules after all, says Okamoto-san.

Meanwhile, Okamoto-san points out that living in Malaysia doesn't mean that Japanese people can make a lot of money, because if they earn yen from their respective companies in Japan, of course, they would have to pay taxes to the Japanese government. Okamoto-san, for his part, reveals that he earns in ringgit, the Malaysian currency, and saves in order to secure his own financial future.

Finally, Okamoto-san shares with us that he's been in the hospital for two days. He's undergoing his last medical exam on this day, and will be already leaving the following day.
