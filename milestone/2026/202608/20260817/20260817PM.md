First, Okamoto-san tells us that perhaps people who listen to his talk would not anymore have the interest to read the book themselves, although Okamoto-san clarifies that what he shares with us includes his own thoughts about this topic, which is related to capitalism.

Okamoto-san says that Mr. Piketty teaches that global wealth needs to be redistributed. He also explains that after researching 300 years of world economies in terms of income and assets, he found that people, who've accumulated considerable wealth, own assets in which they've invested previously. Furthermore, this means that only increasing income doesn't help people accumulate wealth, because they should also invest a percentage of their earnings in buying assets.

Next, Okamoto-san gives the example of Christians, who, it's said, contribute 10% of their income to the Church as donation. Meanwhile, governments of the world, for their part, impose taxes on their citizens. This is why there are people who look for ways to avoid paying these taxes, and, as a result, have accumulated huge wealth. Moreover, this wealth appears to remain in the same group of rich people for generations. Hence, according to Okamoto-san, Mr. Piketty advises the redistribution of global wealth.

In addition, Okamoto-san shares with us that while he agrees with the importance of striving in order to accumulate wealth, the same with what Mr. Piketty says, he doesn't want people to stop working, nor children commit crimes to be able to eat bread. However, in places like Africa, it's very strange that people don't want to be independent after receiving plenty of financial aid for decades.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that although he thinks that there will always be ways to avoid paying taxes, he believes that it's necessary to raise the morality of poor countries, in addition to redistributing global wealth.
