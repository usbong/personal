Sakai-san recounts that he began by playing the guitar. The "Morris W-25" was the first instrument that he had bought, though he discovered that there were sounds that would be better played with the piano. In those days, he still didn't own a piano, so during his studies at the university, he borrowed a music room to learn to play the piano for a month, because he found a music school that needed someone who could play the piano for student vocalists. Sakai-san reveals that he was able to create up to over 40 songs, and received 6,000 yen as payment, a rather inexpensive amount, although he admits that he enjoyed playing the piano so much and was able to do it freely every morning at the music room.

After graduating from university in 1983, he was looking for available work on the magazines when he found an ad that wanted someone to create music, hence, Sakai-san entered Date East whose senior sound creator would leave the company just a month after his arrival. Nonetheless, Sakai-san says that he worked with another person, who was a part-timer. The company also told him that the "Master Up" or the final game would be release every only three weeks, though little by little he found that he could create music in a period of one year, which let him enjoy the work without doing any overtime at all.

Much later, in 1996, Sakai-san transferred to HAL Laboratory, which was using an Intranet in which people could communicate with each other via chat boxes, even though they were very near each other, something that he admits to not being able to do very well at the start. Sakai-san further explains that during that time, he needed the person to visit him in order to listen to the music or Sakai-san would have to send him the cassette tape.
 
This is all. Of course, there's much more to say, but I should already end here this part of the conversation between Obata-san and Sakai-san.
