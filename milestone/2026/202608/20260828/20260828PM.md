First, Okamoto-san introduces us the scenario wherein a curry restaurant chain called Massaman Curry would give us the recipe for its prized curry if we would invest 5 million yen in it. According to Okamoto-san, the ROI (Return on Investment) is 20% after 5 years; therefore, we'll be getting 6 million yen in total after 5 years.

Okamoto-san next asks Ms. Tapi if she'd invest, and she responds that, in short, no. Okamoto-san, for his part, explains that while he very much likes Massaman Curry's curry, which has red as well as green colors, he thinks that given that he doesn't have 5 million yen right now, such that he'd need to borrow from the bank to obtain this money, the earnings that he'd get would drop to only 4%, because we'll always have to also factor in the interest that the bank takes.

Further still, Okamoto-san answers a question from Ms. Tapi by saying that this curry isn't yet sold in Japan for the same reasons that other food products such as Nata de Coco aren't anymore sold in Japan. While Okamoto-san reiterates that their curry is very delicious and a bit sweeter than other types of curries, he doesn't want to put his house as collateral in order to obtain 5 million yen, in addition to the risk that the business wouldn't succeed in the end. Therefore, Okamoto-san prefers to make money on the S&P stock market at a rate of 8.5% or higher. 

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that while, the same with Ms. Tapi, he doesn't want to invest in this curry business, he's going to introduce us to more case studies in this series about businesses.
