First, Okamoto-san reiterates that he's very fascinated with vintage jeans, saying that their design changes along with the passage of time, such that the pants gradually become slimmer near the bottom after being only cut straight originally.

In addition, Okamoto-san gives reasons for this change, pointing out that the jeans had been initially created, so that there would be pants that wouldn't easily tear when the workers, particularly, the miners, went to work.

Okamoto-san also talks about the Levi's line of jeans called Big E, which used for the first time the Levi's brand in capital letters. He also recalls another line of pants (from another company) called Nikkapokka, which had a distinct style in that the part near the buttocks was larger, while the part near the legs was slimmer. Okamoto-san explains that this type of design was more comfortable to wear for people who rode the motorcycle. Moreover, Okamoto-san points out that the designs and products that people don't like are what get promoted. Fashion is indeed such.

Furthermore, the prices of vintage products from 50 or more years ago could cost people millions of yen, and Okamoto-san wonders if the buyers would really wear jeans that cost so much. For his part, he answers that he'd want to wear old and dirty jeans.

Next, Okamoto-san adds other vintage things such as Volkswagen vehicles like the Beetle or the Van. He also talks about a tiny car from England that he used to own previously, and which had an engine behind the driver's seat. However, this engine would become hot, so much so that Okamoto-san's back would also become hot while he tried to drive it.

Finally, Okamoto-san answers his viewers' questions. For instance, he talks about the arcade games that he liked, though he admits that they would break the players' will. He gives examples of games like Xevious, Gradius, Twin Bee, Samurai Spirits, Tetris as well as Virtua Fighter. He also says that if he could return to the past with the knowledge that he has now, he'd like to create these games himself.

There's much more to say in Okamoto-san's talk, of course, but I should already stop here.
