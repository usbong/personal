First, Okamoto-san recounts that from his recollection, jeans began to appear in the 1840s when gold miners needed a new style of pants that didn't rip easily when they went down to the mining sites to work; thus, the company, Levi's, launched a new line of pants called 501 that had buttons in the part near the zipper. 

Furthermore, after the second world war, the same company, Levi's, launched another new line of pants called 501XX. Okamoto-san admits that the pants cost, and up to now still cost a lot, as much as 3 million yen per pair, such that it isn't easy for him to get hold of them. He also explains that the pants have a straight design instead of getting slimmer going toward the bottom. Also, they have a unique sewing design, which, according to Okamoto-san, was made, because the sewing machines at the time still couldn't achieve what the designers originally wanted.

Okamoto-san next talks about P-san, who is a connoisseur of jeans. He says that he's seen him in a program buying several jeans in London. Okamoto-san shares with us that vintage pants have faded color characteristics, and knows that there are people who want to create this type of design with stains using new pants, but he thinks that it's a deceit, because he prefers something natural that gradually occurs through the passage of time. According to Okamoto-san's explanation, in fact, Levi's pants are only washed once or twice a year to have their distinct look.

Okamoto-san then reveals that he loves Levi's jeans, because they look good, especially when he wears them. Also, he points out that in Thailand people can find very cheap jeans, because they're shipped from the US in large piles including Levi's pants. However, Okamoto-san says that the pants that he can put on are quite expensive, because his size is much smaller than the one that people in the US wear.

Finally, Okamoto-san answers questions from his viewers, saying that he no longer owns shares of his former studio, Capcom, because he doesn't want to be accused of using insider information from his friends who're still at Capcom. Moreover, he mentions that he wants to try out Tokon Fighting Souls, the new fighting game from Arc System Works, because he's been told that it's a game that greatly respects the Capcom fighting game series that he had led and produced previously.
