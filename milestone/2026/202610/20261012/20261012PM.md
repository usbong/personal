First, Okamoto-san explains that a person who's thinking about owning the shares of a company has to consider three things: the rise in the value of the shares, the dividends, and the special treatment that he can receive for owning the shares.

On this point, Okamoto-san highlights that the last one about special treatment can only be taken advantage of if the person currently lives in Japan, so he thinks that investors including himself who live outside Japan forfeit this unique service.

Okamoto-san then recounts that when he still owned the shares of Oriental Land, he would get six tickets in a year to enter the amusement park. Although he just would give out those tickets to other people, an owner could actually sell them to get money in exchange as well. 

Okamoto-san next gives more examples of the companies that, he thinks, provide very interesting special treatments. One is Japan's McDonald's Holdings that gives out three types of discount tickets twice each year. One is for the hamburger, another is for the side dish like salads, and the last is for the drinks. Also, the minimum requirement to get these discounts is to invest 700,000 yen.

Of course, one must also own other shares of other companies, because, as Okamoto-san explains, the special treatment doesn't improve very much, even though a person had invested more. That is, if a person owns 100 shares, he'd get one point, and when he owns 1,000 shares, he won't necessarily get 10 points.

Another example is NTT, which gives out d-points that are valued at a maximum of 4,500 yen upon owning at least 20,000 yen of shares. Another is Ajinomoto, which gives out the company's product valued at 500 yen.

Okamoto-san then points out that just like investor Mr. Kiritani, who has garned a lot of attention in Japan in this way, a person could also get several special treatments by owning the minimum amount necessary of the shares of various companies. Of course, in the case of Mr. Kiritani, because he's achieved Financial Independence Retire Early (FIRE), he now lives in a very interesting way, waiting for what he'd get each day.
