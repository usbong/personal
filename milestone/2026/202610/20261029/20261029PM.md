Okamoto-san begins by clarifying that, of course, wanting to live in a city as a still unrealized goal and wanting to continue living there are two different things.

With this in mind, Okamoto-san asks Ms. Tapi about the number one city in which she wants to live. She answers that she'd like to live in Fukuoka, a city that has delicious food as well as cheap housing rents. In addition, it's very close to an international airport. Ms. Tapi also mentions that she's originally from Fukuoka, her place of birth.

Okamoto-san, for his part, agrees, revealing that Fukuoka is actually the people's most favorite city, also adding that there are many beautiful women there.

Okamoto-san next asks Ms. Tapi the second city in which she wants to live. She answers that maybe it's Yokohama, a city, according to Okamoto-san, very close to Tokyo, although it has a different vibe. Furthermore, the bullet train passes by the city. Meanwhile, Ms. Tapi emphasizes the city's port.

However, this time around, Okamoto-san informs her that Yokohama is actually the people's third most favorite city, because the second is Naha City in Okinawa.

Okamoto-san admits that he's thought about living in Naha before, saying that it's a place very close to the sea as well as it's for people who don't worry about getting a tan. However, he reveals that he thinks that it's not a city for businessmen, who after opening a shop, would get comments from people who'd tell them that if only they also had the money, they'd go start a business of their own too.

Moreover, Okamoto-san points out that the city receives a lot of financial support from Japan and the U.S. for allowing U.S. military bases there, so the people of Naha City don't really need to strive very much. Okamoto-san also says that they would continue to survive without the need to worry. However, because of this, they don't often arrive on time and are very laid-back.
