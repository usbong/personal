Okamoto-san begins by saying that nowadays acquiring only 100 million yen's worth of assets isn't anymore enough to achieve FIRE, pointing out that he knows women who've done it in their 30s or 40s. Furthermore, given that the value of the Japanese Yen is currently low, an amount of 100 million yen won't be the same as 1 million dollars.

Moreover, the prices of goods in the U.S. have risen. This is why Okamoto-san advises aiming for an amount higher than 100 million yen like 300 million yen. In fact, the women that Okamoto-san mentions aim for 300 million yen and another amount for their personal insurance.

Okamoto-san next reiterates ways like the S&P to get some 8% of earnings through investment, while reminding us to take into account Japanese taxes amounting to 20%. Also, while there are people who're very capable in lowering their expenses, Okamoto-san admits that he can't do this very well. However, he explains that there are actually four concrete ways to achieve FIRE. They are: further increasing income, lowering down lifestyle-related expenses, working longer, and investing wisely.

Okamoto-san calculates that a person who earns 4 million yen every year could acquire enough assets to achieve FIRE.

To end, Okamoto-san says that he also wants to read our comments about this topic.

Therefore, I'd like to say that I've met people who give the excuse that they steal money to get food that they then give to their family. They also point out that rich bosses don't pay correct taxes to the governement either, so they believe that it's not bad to steal or cheat in order to survive. However, when we check their families, we'd find that they have members who don't arrive on time, play mobile games instead of study, change schools more than once, can't find stable jobs, and smoke cigarettes or vape. 

Further still, since they seem to be able to get by with their mother stealing, the mother now also belongs to a group that routinely borrows money without the intention of paying it in full, waiting for the old person whom she and the other members have borrowed it from to die and returning only a portion if it's demanded of them.
