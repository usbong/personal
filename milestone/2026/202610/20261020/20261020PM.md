> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: The conversation with Senji Ishii, the ex-editor of monthly magazine GAMEST

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-10-21

## --

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture three years ago about his conversation with Mr. Senji Ishii, the ex-editor of GAMEST, a monthly video game magazine.

First, Ishii-san recounts that he was a writer for GAMEST, and since only three people, who didn't understand very well hardcore games, were at the publisher at the time, little by little, a person like himself began to look at and edit articles that they would publish. He also admits that they at GAMEST were previously writers of VG2, another video game magazine. Furthermore, while Ishii-san didn't name the magazine, "GAMEST," he loved the name. Okamoto-san recalls that the word GAMEST was from GAME, afterwhich, GAMER, until finally, GAMEST, meaning "the best gamer," so he loved in particular the idea of gamers coming together to form a group in order to create something together.

Ishii-san next recalls that during those years, while there were already studios such as TAITO, NAMCO and SEGA, CAPCOM hadn't been so well known yet. However, it appeared so suddenly and released titles so well-made that magazine editors like Ishii-san noticed it. 

Ishii-san also shares that he was able to get an interview with three great people at CAPCOM: Mr. Takashi Nishiyama, Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto and Mr. Tokuro Fujiwara. Also, he recalls that Nishiyama-san was like Fujiwara-san in that he liked the feeling of overcoming challenges that were very difficult and gave a lot of stress, also pointing out that Nishiyama-san focused on the action and how it could be felt. Meanwhile, he says that Okamoto-san preferred to play games without a lot of stress.

Ishii-san also reveals that the first strategy guide that he had written was for Makaimura (or Ghosts n' Goblins), saying that a non-expert gamer could actually finish it after trying many times. However, as Okamoto-san says, newbies who aren't video game writers probably wouldn't play it as much, emphasizing the need to pay 100 yen and to spend the time in order to play it at the arcades.

Moreover, Ishii-san and Okamoto-san speak about games like Gun.Smoke and 1943, which were games that Okamoto-san had created at Capcom. Both admit that they haven't finished it up to the end because of its very intense difficulty. On the other hand, another game, Magic Sword, had been a game that Okamoto-san was able to finish and one that Ishii-san and his team wrote a guide too. 

In addition, another game that Ishii-san speaks about is Rush & Crash, which was a car game and which he thought to be very amusing, but was too challenging due to the controls, so much so that, in fact, it didn't turn into a bestseller. Nonetheless, he recalls the team at Capcom explaining to him that they had created it to be precisely so, something completely different from the review of Ishii-san, who afterward felt very much the will of the developers to create it.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes by mentioning that Ishii-san didn't like to write articles about fighting games, to which he answers that it was because this type of game had been more for gamers in the U.S. instead of Japan, although Okamoto-san points out that the Japanese really played it a lot inside the studio.
