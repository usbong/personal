First, Ms. Tapi reports that attacks from bears in Japan seem to have risen. Okamoto-san responds that it's probably because they're looking for food that they couldn't get in the mountains, also giving examples of things such as bells and sprays that are said to have the smell that bears dislike, and thus could be used to defend against these animals.

Next, Tapi-san shares that a rugby athlete has died of heatstroke, pointing out that nowadays the air-conditioner is now a necessity in order to survive against the extremely hot weather in Japan. She also reveals that she's gotten herself vaccinated against the flu, which is transmitted even during hot weather. 

With respect to this point, Okamoto-san explains that a vaccine is efficacious for only three months, afterwhich the person has to get vaccinated again, also mentioning that each person has his own blood type, such that various vaccines exist depending on the case and the person.

Okamoto-san further clarifies that the flu is known to get transmitted more rapidly when the temperature is low, but if the immune system of the person goes down because of the hot weather, of course, flu gets transmitted to that person rather easily as well. Therefore, we must value balance in terms of the environment, exercise as well as food, among other factors.

Finally, Tapi-san recounts that there have been breaches in the embankment against the Chiba dam in three places due to the typhoon in September. Okamoto-san recalls that in Mobara City in Chiba, where he used to live, there were places nearby where the cars had been inundated because of a typhoon, and the sewage system in these places that were less than five meters above sea level couldn't work fast enough. Furthermore, Okamoto-san foresees that there wil surely be another typhoon in Chiba at the start of November, so a person should always take into account these factors when he chooses a place where he'd want to live.
