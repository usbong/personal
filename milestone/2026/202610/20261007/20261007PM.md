Okamoto-san shares that after a surge in camping, its popularity is now waning. He explains that it's because, to be honest, it isn't a lot of fun, particularly the part after, which is cleaning up. Moreover, the routine is generally only setting up the tent, cooking, eating, cleaning up, and repeat. Okamoto-san also points out that you have to pay for the equipment, including the tent.

However, on the other hand, Okamoto-san highlights that it's very relaxing to leave the house and look at other sceneries. There's also fun in cooking our food while surrounded by nature, although the food might not really be that delicious.

Okamoto-san then reveals that because he doesn't like the part about cleaning up, he's bought a Mercedes-Benz Gerende customized for use in camping. The SUV had cost him 10 million yen and up, but his point is that we can change what we don't like, so that we'd enjoy the experience of camping as well.

Finally, Okamoto-san encourages us to also share our thoughts about this topic.

To respond, I'd like to say that in the 90s when I was still in high school, I was able to experience with my family, including my relatives, making a camp on the beach. We also made a bonfire, and since I had brought a Sony Discman, previously owned by my older brother, we were able to listen to the music of Rurouni Kenshin, particularly Mr. Noriyuki Asakura's "KAMIYA KAORU," which was included in the CD that my younger brother and I had bought at Comic Alley in Virra Mall, Greenhills.
