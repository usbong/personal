> 80% of public hospitals in Japan aren't making money, what are the "last citadels" in the policy necessary to solve this problem? (Part 1 of 2)

> Translator: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-10-08

## Last Updated: 2026-10-09:11:51

## --

The mismanagement of public hospitals in Japan is a deep topic. According to a study made by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, in 2024, 80% of the 844 hospitals all over Japan have lost a total of 395.2 billion yen. In general, public hospitals are managed by the Local Government Units (LGU) that use a special form of accounting; however, even if money is infused above the budget, it still won't be enough. Furthermore, the direction in which things are going is said to remain the same for the year 2025.

One big reason why mismanagement is worsening is, first and foremost, due to the increase in the cost of human resources. Moreover, the low value of the Japanese currency, along with inflation, is pulling up the prices of all medical equipment like gauzes and IV tubes. While the base income in the compensation for medical staff is rising by a two-year average of 3.09% from 2026 to 2027, this can't catch up with the costs. Of course, this is also impacted by the finances of the LGU itself, which is unable to compensate accordingly. 

If losses continue, there might be reductions in the number of staff and the medical services available. When aging facilities are likewise considered, the worst case scenario would be: hospitals would have no other way but to shut down.
