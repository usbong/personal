First, Okamoto-san admits that he keeps on telling us that we should save money, but for what things should we really use the money that we've saved?

He gives three things.

The first thing that Okamoto-san advises is to set aside 3 to 6 months-worth of money in cash. He explains that it's for any emergency that may occur.

The second is to use the money to experience what we currently can. He says that now that he's 65 years old, leaving the house to travel as a backpacker is already very difficult for him to do, especially because his knees sometimes would hurt. This is why he advises young people to do what they'd want with the money while they're still not old.

The third is to invest in ourselves as well as in the stock market or the new NISA by the Japanese government, which, he says, would give people enough money to turn it into their own pension when they've gotten older. Okamoto-san also emphasizes that the Japanese who didn't know the S&P stock market had invested in the Japanese currency, which has gone down 35%, and given that the prices of goods have also risen, they have lost up to 45%.

Finally, Okamoto-san advises young people to save as early as possible, set aside a portion for emergency, experience what they currently can, and invested in themselves as well as in the stock market or Japan's new NISA.

Before closing, Okamoto-san says that he wants to read our comments about this topic, so I'd like to say that knee pain is a problem that affects old people as well as the young, depending on their lifestyle, of course. Nowadays, I eat red meat every day, a slice of Mozzarrella cheese once every one or two weeks, and a big orange for the evening,  in addition to doing exercises including light jogging every morning to solve this problem, although I know that other people may probably need another regimen.
