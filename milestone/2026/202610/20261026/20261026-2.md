> Will Japan lift its importation of raw potatoes because of US pressure? Varying views amid the risk of pests and diseases

> Translator: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-10-26

## --

Key Points

## --

Risk of Pests and Diseases: This is the local producers' biggest concern. It was in 2015 when Abashiri City in Hokkaido first confirmed having detected the Potato White Cyst Nematode. Pest extermination is still ongoing more than 10 years later. 

Key Considerations by the Japanese Government: There are varying views: One is to not lift the ban if no control mechanisms can be made to be able to stop the pests from infesting the farmlands. Another is to yield to US demands and lift the ban.
 
## --

Timeline of Events

## --

2006: Japan lifts the ban on the importation of potatoes used only for processed potato chips. The US has since then requested Japan to make a complete lift of the ban.

2020: The US requests Japan to lift the ban on the importation of raw potatoes.

September 2026: The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries announces the need to have risk management measures in the importation of raw potatoes, identifying 21 specific varieties of pests. The third stage in the process to lift the ban is the last, in which Japan is to meet with the US side to discuss the details of the policy to work around the risk.

## --

What kinds of risk?

## --

Pests: Among the 21 varieties of pests detected, the farmers are particularly concerned about the "Potato Cyst Nematode" and the "Potato White Cyst Nematode." 

Impact: While the pests aren't harmful to the human body as long as they are only 1 mm long, if they parasite the root of the potatoes, the amount of yield decreases by 50%. They're also strong, living longer than 15 years even inside soil that is dry or have low temperatures, thereby making them difficult to eradicate.

Limiting factor: In the farmlands where they grow, cultivation of normal potatoes, among other varieties, are restricted.

## --

Reaction of Raw Producers?

## --

The fight against pests: In 2015, farmers at Abashiri City in Hokkaido, where the "Potato White Cyst Nematode" was first confirmed, reported, "We suddenly couldn't cultivate potatoes." Income is said to have also decreased in the millions of yen. 

From other farmers: "When pests come and infest the farmlands, we won't be able to produce potatoes for decades," "Isn't it impossible to completely control pests that are invisible?"

## --

What is the Ministry's explanation?

## --

Discussions with the US: According to international law, while discussion itself cannot be refused, it doesn't mean that the ban will be lifted.

Key consideration: From a scientific point of view, and factoring in the complete removal of the infested soil, Japan's lifting of the ban is based on whether or not the US side can take measures to prevent pest infestation.

## --

Giving in to US demands?

## --

The University of Tokyo Special Appointed Professor Nobuhiro Suzuki: We see that we're moving toward the lifting of the importation ban next year in accordance with the US' request. Japan's rate of production of potatoes in 2024 was 66%, indicating the marked decrease in the amount of raw production of potatoes after local production was hit by the pests.

## --

Meiji University's Professor Takumi Sakuyama: If the US side doesn't provide sufficient pest control measures, we see discussions further continuing, and the ban still being kept in place.

## --

The situation in other countries?

## --

South Korea, Taiwan: These countries import raw potatoes produced by the US. The Ministry says, "Both countries haven't obtained information that pests have infested and have spread in their farmlands."

Mexico: In 2014, local raw producers complained in court because of the risk of pest infestation, thereby stopping the importation of potatoes produced by the US temporarily. Nationwide distribution began in 2022 through the decision made by the Supreme Court, among other things.
