Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture about Mr. Rokusuke Ei, who said the phrase, "Humans die twice."

## --

Okamoto-san begins by saying that he wants to introduce to us Ei-san, a Japanese writer who, unfortunately, has already passed away, but is, nevertheless, also a great person whom Okamoto-san respects very much, pointing out his phrase about death.

He explains that the first time that a person dies is of a biological death, while the second time is when the memories about him have already been forgotten.

Okamoto-san points out that it's actually the same with video games in that they cease to exist when all the people who had played it have already died.

He next recounts that when animals die, we count what remains, for instance, in the Japanese language, how many heads of the cow, the pig, or the sheep, while it's the feather for the birds. As for human beings, what remains, according to Okamoto-san, is the name, which when people have already forgotten it, that person dies the second time.

Thus, Okamoto-san says that he strives to share with people his memories, which he hopes would be able to help them. Also, he admits that he had thought about whether it was better to write a book or launch videos, and he decided in the end that the latter would be more economical.

Okamoto-san also shares that he recalls his grandmother when he speaks with his mom, particularly, about food. While his grandmother has already died and his mom is now 88 years old, while he's 65, his grandmother is still alive in his memory. However, he reveals that he almost doesn't anymore recall his great-grandparents who, the same with his grandmother, were cremated.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that he doesn't want people to remember him for criminal offenses, so he strives to help them by being a guide like a street sign.

Before ending, Okamoto-san advises us to write our thoughts as well, so I'd like to say that I realized the importance of the contribution made by people, even more than the name of the person who did it when I saw the video from PBS Food on "[Where Did American BBQ Really Come From?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CUCjnvBf1vA)," emphasizing more what I can learn from the person and how I can apply it in my present life. Finally, I believe that names, including details in the story, can be changed with the passage of time in order to serve a purpose, the same with what the Catholic Church, among others, do.
