> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: Recollections about working in the video game industry 45 years ago

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-11-01

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture about his recollections when he began to work in the video game industry 45 years ago.

## --

Okamoto-san recounts that he entered KONAMI in those years as his first job in the industry. He recalls that he worked on creating his first game called "Time Pilot," wherein the jet fighter pilot went to battle in three epoches, in which each one would introduce unique enemies.

However, he reveals that this game had not been what his sempai (older colleague) had told him to create, so much so that he went so far as to tell Okamoto-san to resign from the studio. Okamoto-san, for his part, explains that this colleague wass only a year older than him, and he thought that he as well as the other older colleagues didn't strive as much. This is why he answered him that he created it in this way in order to be more fun, although he needed to strive more in order to finish the project.

Next, the second game that Okamoto-san created was "Gyruss", another shooting game. He says that, this time around, the jet fighter pilot had to shoot toward the center where the enemies would appear, while he moved in circles. Furthermore, the game was inspired by Galaga, the NAMCO game that Okamoto-san liked so much that he wanted to create one like it with improvements. 

In addition, he worked with another older colleague, Mr. Toshio Arima, the programmer who would also move to CAPCOM later. Moreover, the owner of KONAMI, Mr. Kagemasa Koduki, told him to add a classic melody arranged as a rock music. He also recalls that the sound team worked hard, even puting a specialized sound hardware to be able to play the melody. Because of this, Okamoto-san was able to understand the value of music, especially because there was still no music like this previously. As a result, this game, just like Okamoto-san's first game, was able to sell well, something that really surprised him.

Finally, Okamoto-san thanks KONAMI, because if it hadn't existed, there won't be the Okamoto of today as well.

This is all. I end here Okamoto-san's story, although there's still a second part where he tells more about what happened after he moved from KONAMI to the new CAPCOM studio. He also wants to read our comments if we've played any of his games during that period.
