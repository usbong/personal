Okamoto-san tells us that foreigners buy old houses costing 5 million yen or less. In fact, especially in the provinces, they would cost only from 1 to 3 million yen. Afterward, they then renovate them, repairing the roofs, cultivating its small plot of land, among other things. 

Okamoto-san explains that the Japanese don't do this, because they prefer modern architecture, pointing out that the old houses aren't so durable to withstand both hot and cold weather, as well as earthquakes. Also, only asking the renovator would already cost a lot of money. This is why Okamoto-san agrees that the value of a house is reduced up to 0 yen after 25 years, so the buyer would really only pay for the land.

However, Okamoto-san would still want to renovate houses, so much so that he reminds Ms. Tapi to call him if she would again buy a house, because she just finished renovating one of their family's costing 4 million yen. Also, Ms. Tapi recounts that if she's going to buy a house, she thinks that it would be in Kobe, where her older brother, who prefers a provincial environment, would live.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that Ms. Tapi could get rent from the house that they would build DIY-style for her older brother, who, in turn, would also get a good value for a cheap price.
