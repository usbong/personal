> Core Member of Qilin Ransomware Group accountable for "actively attacking" businesses detained in Osaka

> Translator: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-10-06

## --

A core member of the Qilin Ransomware Group widely known for their repeated cyberattacks against businesses all over the world was detained by Japanese investigators. Japanese authorities have already handed over the 28-year-old Russian male, who is being suspected of extortion, to their German counterparts in accordance with the "Extradition Act."

In 2025, the Asahi Group Holdings company suffered cyberattacks, which included Denial-of-Service attacks, from the Qilin group. Together with other nations, Japanese and German investigators have been jointly performing investigations on these attacks.

According to sources, the man accessed in September 2024 the terminal of a logistics company based in Germany, and encrypted the data that he had illegaly acquired. He's being suspected of making threats and demanding that if the company didn't want the data to be divulged to the public, they must pay about 26 million yen in Bitcoin currency.

Qilin publishes information about the businesses and organizations that they've attacked on a "Leak Site," where, very recently, tens and hundreds of posts per month are beng uploaded. They are considered to be "one of the most active large-scale cyberattack groups" that's also been growing globally, says Ms. Mika Fukuda of the "Mitsui & Co. Secure Directions (MBSD)."

MBSD has also reported that in August of this year, Qilin has made 161 posts on their Leak Site. Furthermore, among the 370 or so cyberattack groups that the company is watching, they are the group with the highest number of posts. In September, there were 75 cyberattack posts, the second highest this year.

With respect to Japanese businesses and organizations, Qilin has launched attacks against the overseas branches of giant automobile parts makers and private universities since the start of this year with 14 posts published on the Leak Site.

Based on a survey given to businesses and others who've suffered these cyberattacks, about 50%, the highest, indicated that the route where ransomware infected them was through the VPN (Virtual Private Network).

The police say that the group that develops ransomware and runs the business provides the ransomware to the actual cyberattackers, and takes a portion of the ransom money as part of its "Ransomware as a Service (RaaS)." Even without high-end technology and specialized knowledge, the attacks could be made, leading to an increase in the number of cyberattackers. Qilin is also considered to be a criminal group that runs RaaS.
