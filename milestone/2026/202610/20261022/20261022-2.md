> I.T. Pro Stories: Why I scrapped my I.T. thesis proposal on "The Business of Managing an Online Electronic Medical Records System for Patients in the Philippines While Based in Singapore"

> Translator: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-10-22

## --

Dear friends,

Greetings of peace.

In light of the [news](https://digital.asahi.com/articles/ASVBP4CXWVBPUTIL02TM.html?pn=5&unlock=1#continuehere) from Asahi News about two major vulnerabilities found in the PHP Programming Language that prompted several web administrators in various countries in Europe to momentarily stop serving content in order to upgrade their respective system's PHP versions, I'd like to share with you the reason why I opted to scrap my I.T. thesis proposal on "The Business of Managing an Online Electronic Medical Records System for Patients in the Philippines While Based in Singapore." 

First and foremost, let me note that I knew beforehand that I would need someone with sufficient I.T. training, preferably [PhilNITS-certified](https://philnits.org/), and based in the Philippines to assist me in checking our local computer server from time to time. However, I also knew that it would be extremely difficult to find someone who'd be up to the task, especially because those who could already have jobs.

My plan was to migrate the existing server from local to online using Amazon Web Services (AWS), a service that I've been using since over a decade ago. I'd then create another server in Singapore to be used for diagnosing and fixing problems that the clinic staff based in the Philippines would report before I update the "prod (production)" version online accordingly, just like what I've been doing locally. 

I've also created and prepared my own encryption tools to make sure that the database file, which would include patient data, would be encrypted before it's transferred from the Philippines to Singapore and back using time-scheduled tasks that run scripts like the batch files on Windows. 

I recall that using phpmyadmin to access the database in AWS is possible, something that I was doing over five years ago when I was managing my own online bookstore; however, equally possible is the reality that criminal organizations including top-tier smugglers view online traffic data using tools like wireshark. We now know that they do more than just view. They also modify data, among other types of mischief.

But what really stopped me from pursuing this thesis is the fact that blackouts and Internet Service Provider (ISP)-related issues that require the computers and the routers to be restarted occur rather frequently in the Philippines. 

When blackouts occur, the server, along with the other computers in the network, has to be manually turned on again. While this occurs rather rarely because the computers have been pre-configured to turn on automatically, they do occur and there's no other clear way to solve it.

As for the routers, whenever our ISP makes an update related to our non-static public Internet Protocol (IP) address, the routers also have to be restarted, because some of the wireless devices like the tablet PCs won't be able to connect to the wireless printer to print the forms. We've also found that using various software like Rakuten Viber that provide Virtual Private Network (VPN) security could at times cause some of the wireless devices connected to the network to go invisible and cannot even be pinged, of which the solution is to restart the router to give all the devices connected to it a new set of IP addresses.

In the end, I've opted to forgo the business of managing an online electronic medical records system due to the issues that I've explained. I also wouldn't recommend managing any type of online business that soley makes money out of the buying and selling of products online for the long term, and instead would advice supporting well-established businesses that already make money offline.

This is all.
