Ms. Tapi begins by saying that AI has advanced very rapidly, to which Okamoto-san responds that the use of AI is now currently available both paid and free. He further gives the example of Sony's PlayStation 3, which when it came out, people said that the 3D models were already almost the same as the real ones. This is actually the same comment that they said when the PlayStation 4 came out, and the PlayStation 5 after it. Thus, it's foreseen that the interaction with AI will further improve just like Okamoto-san's conversation with Ms. Tapi. Also, of course, there'll be the issue about security in which AI will be used by malicious groups to launch rockets that are controlled by computer systems, which AI agents can enter without permission.

Tapi-san next reports that shady jobs have trapped Japanese people who're looking for ways to make money online. Okamoto-san answers that it's very challenging to get out from this especially when the person has submitted an application including information about his identity such as his address, because the managers of these shady jobs would tell him that they already have his information, frightening him even more. Moreover, the applicant would be afraid of divulging this issue to his parents.

Tapi-san also says that the number of people from Thailand and Cambodia who've disappeared has increased. She says that the cases are related to scams including romance scams. Okamoto-san replies that women of these countries are lured by a beautiful woman with a caucasian man, and if they tried to escape, they would get killed. Okamoto-san also admits that the Japanese tend to trust easily, and he prefers that they increase their defenses by locking the door as well as taking care when they're outside the house. Okamoto-san also mentions that in his province of Ainancho, the inhabitants of the town don't often hear the news that a son of an inhabitant has transferred her 40 million yen, so he says that one has to transfer the money first before calling the mom to avoid the doubt that it's a scam.

In addition, Tapi-san speaks about Mt. Fuji in which adding a fee to rescue hikers is currently being discussed, giving the example of Yamanashi prefecture where people must pay 8,000 yen every 5 minutes of helicopter use. Okamoto-san reveals that he hasn't climbed Mt. Fuji, though he knows young people who've done it, and says that the oxygen goes down as the hiker climbs higher, so much so that he has to wear an oxygen mask.

Finally, Ms. Tapi points out that bicycle rules have become stricter, giving examples like not allowing earphones, in addition to mobile phones, to be used, among other tings. Okamoto-san explains that, basically, these rules refer to the ones for automobiles, so people must yield a distance of 1.5 meters to cyclists. Okamoto-san also emphasizes the importance of wearing a helmet, which children wear when they ride the bike, but they stop this practice when they've gotten older.

In summary, while the year 2026 isn't yet over, Ms. Tapi explains that she's indicated the news reports that appear to her to be very extraordinary. Okamoto-san, for his part, also advises us to comment about this topic.
