> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: The case of Mr. "Donfan" (Kosuke Nozaki) of Kishu and his inheritance amounting to 1.3 billion yen

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-10-15

## --

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture with Ms. Tapi about the case of Mr. "Donfan" (Kosuke Nozaki) of Kishu. Mr. Donfan had left behind 1.3 billion yen to Tanabe City in Wakayama Prefecture according to a suspicious letter after dying due to acute stimulant intoxication. Furthermore, his ex-wife is suspected of murdering him, though, for lack of sufficient evidence, the court has now ruled that she really didn't do it.

As it is, Okamoto-san also points out that the letter is very curious, because the characters that are normally written in the Kanji alphabet had been written in another Japanese alphabet, which is Katakana. Ms. Tapi also explains that if the ex-wife is guilty of forging the letter, other relatives would surely receive the inheritance amounting to billions of yen. If not, as what is written in the letter, Tanabe City will receive it.

Okamoto-san then recalls that his old aunt who, he found, had a substantial inheritance and who moved to his mom's house to be looked after when she had gotten very old, wrote a letter like a will to specify who would receive which parts of her inheritance; however, her handwriting was very difficult to understand especially in her later years, and little by little the amount given to some of their relatives and family members decreased over time.

Finally, Okamoto-san encourages us to write our thoughts about this topic as well.

I'd like to say that, of course, in any family whose members love one another, the parents would want to give each one the same amount of inheritance. However, the reality isn't necessarily so, also because the real world tends to be more favorable toward men in terms of income. Still, in a wealthy family, there will always be members who've gotten used to spending money, so much so that any amount will never be enough.
