> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: The Three Big Rules of Human Life

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-11-24

## --

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's conversation with Tanu-chan about the three big rules of human life.

## --

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-vT2s0ly0FQ; last accessed: 20261123

> 人間の3大原則[人はどう生きるべき]かを問う

## --

Okamoto-san explains that these three rules are: Human beings eventually die, they don't know when, and they have only one life.

Tanu-chan then replies that after hearing this topic, she would like to be grateful to her family and the rest of the people in her life. Also, she points out that she would like to experience what she can in this life by traveling.

Okamoto-san, for his part, reveals that given that he's now 65 years old, there are things that he wants to change, but as far as he knows, we cannot go back to the past, so he emphasizes that we don't be late in doing what we can now.

For example, he communicates with his mom who's 88 years old everyday while he takes walks in the morning, and points out that only 30 minutes of talking on the telephone may be beneficial for both parties. He also recalls that he used to spend 1,000 yen for only 10 minutes to communicate on the telephone during the time when he was outside Japan, and emphasizes that a person could put money in the public telephones in Japan to continue talking with another person, although nowadays they can communicate without paying using apps.

Okamoto-san next recounts that he was only a B-grade student, and he would like to, of course, have been able to strive more instead of relaxing, so because Tanu-chan is 24 years old and is still young, she could do things that an old person like Okamoto can no longer do. With respect to this point, Okamoto-san shares with us that a regret that people who'll already be dying soon is that they didn't challenge themselves to do something that they had wanted since a long time ago such as, for example, marrying a man who has died earlier.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that while it's possible that he might die tomorrow, he doesn't regret living in the way that he has, because it's not a problem for him if he were to die in any part of his life.

This is all. Of course, there are many more things to tell, but I should already stop here. Okamoto-san also wants to read our comments if we have any to say.
