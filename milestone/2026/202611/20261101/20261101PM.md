Okamoto-san recounts that he transferred from KONAMI to the new CAPCOM studio where he would create video games such as Senjo no Okami and Makaimura (Ghosts n' Goblins). He also contacted genius programmers including Tokuro Fujiwara and Hirokazu Fujinaka, along with the assistant, "Harada," from KONAMI to come over to CAPCOM.

Also, Okamoto-san reveals that it was the beginning of CAPCOM, so he worked on various roles, for example, programmer and planner. Furthermore, he recruited part-time workers.

Okamoto-san says that his first work was SONSON whose protagonist was the grandson ("Son" in the Japanese language) of Son Goku, hence "SONSON." Moreover, there was another protagonist called TONTON, who was actually a pig for player 2. It was a shooting game combined with side-scrolling platformer elements.

Okamoto-san admits that the game wasn't able to sell a lot because of reasons like he hadn't considered very much the marketing aspect as well as due to development and manufacturing costs in which the difference between the revenue and these would make up the profit.

Because the development costs would have to be recuperated, it wouldn't yet earn any profit at the moment. However, after this step, the motherboards that they'd manufature would enable them earn a lot. In fact, this was precisely their business model.

With this idea in mind, while the game SONSON could only contribute 1/5 or 1/6 of the sales, Okamoto-san reveals that CAPCOM President Kenzo Tsujimoto didn't complain to him at all, and he thinks that it's probably because the president was anticipating the second or next work that they'd release.

At this point, Okamoto-san mentions that Tokuro Fujiwara launched his first game called "Vargas." He also says that the journalists thought that their two games were very similar to NAMCO's games, something that they were aiming for at the start, because, according to Okamoto-san, they couldn't compete against NINTENDO, so they, who were only 22 years old at the time, used as their benchmark NAMCO instead of NINTENDO. This is why they created icons such as the Yashichi, in addition to vegetables and fruits, in order to build the new studio's brand.

Finally, Okamoto-san reiterates the four conditions to sell video games: The game must be interesting to hear, see, play, and repeat.
