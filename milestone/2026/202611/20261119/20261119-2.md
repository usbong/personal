> Stolen locally produced high-class vehicles to be laundered and sold to criminal organizations? 10 members arrested 

> Author: Mami Okada

> Translator: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-11-18


## --

There's now a report on the status of the locally produced high-class vehicles that have been stolen in Osaka, Kyoto and others. Their vehicle numbers are said to be laundered and the vehicles transferred to criminal organizations. 

The Osaka Prefecture Police have announced on the 18th the arrest of 10 people including both male and female persons in their 20s and 30s as well as a 32-year old male doing logistics operations from the Suminoe Ward in Osaka City for systematically stealing automobiles.

According to Investigation Division 3, the arrest was made after results from investigations showed that the members have stolen a total of 28 locally produced high-class vehicles in Osaka and Kyoto Prefectures from June 2025 to February 2026. The total amount of these stolen vehicles has climbed to about 160 million yen.

Five of the members have been sued for having stolen three of the vehicles amounting to a total of about 13.5 million yen prior to their arrest.

The men bought used vehicles at a flee market site for a cheap price, and then removed the vehicle's engraving with the vehicle number. They then replaced the stolen high-class vehicle's own vehicle number with that one. In addition, they obtained a new plate number to make it look like the vehicle is new.

While the prefecture police were investigating the whereabouts of the stolen vehicles, some were bought by criminal organizations related to violent behavior and drugs. These vehicles are also used by criminal members, according to the police.

Aside from the aforementioned vehicles, the prefecture police also regard the other vehicles to have been planned to be sold to criminal organizations.

One of the suspects has confirmed this: "The stolen vehicles are hidden in a paid parking lot and are planned to be sold days later."

10 of the arrested suspects had been tasked to give orders, carry them out, replace the vehicle numbers, and even dispose of the items inside the vehicles.
