This time around, he shares that he continued playing Street Fighter II  with several rules of his like not using ZANGIEF's attack called SPINNING PILE DRIVER or giving his opponent the opportunity to kick him. He also played with only one hand, although in this case, he admits that he used ZANGIEF's trick with the body slam.

However, he recounts that because Street Fighter II didn't have any tournaments in those days, he tried playing SEGA's Virtua Fighter 2, which had a very popular tournament. Although he reveals that he wasn't able to quickly get used to playing it at the start, in contrast to Street Fighter II, he was able to win with the AKIRA character in the national tournament, which used a variety of arcade machines. He also reveals that he won a year's supply of Asahi beer as well as the "Tetsujin" rank when he was still in his first or second year at university. The latter was to encourage players to achieve the same rank for every character in the game. Moreover, SEGA hired him to participate in another tournament in which 100 players would join. He recalls that he still lived in Kyoto City and had to travel to another city at a distance similar to a trip from Osaka to Okayama, though, in the end, he was able to win 80 times and lost 20 times. All the players had the Tetsujin rank.

He next admits that before joining the tournament, he discovered with his older brother a bug in the game which allowed the player to punch multiple times limitlessly. This bug was called Sembon Punch (Thousand Punches), and which Leo himself named after the Sembon Dori (Thousand Pathways) in Kyoto. Furthermore, he explains that while the opponent could defend himself against this attack, because there was the ringout rule in Virtua Fighter 2, the other player could ultimately push him outside the stage by exploiting the bug. Because of this, he informed SEGA about this bug, because he enjoyed playing the game without ruining its gameplay balance by only exploiting a bug in order to win.

With respect to this point, Okamoto-san shares that the naming is very important, and gives the example of the Kuroneko (Black Cats) team, who named their project as the Shironeko (White Cats) project to give it a sense of being strange. He also recalls that SEGA's arcade machine called NAOMI was named after a very popular female model called Naomi Campbell, athough they didn't include the "Campbell" to avoid the possibility of encountering any problems in the copyright.

Finally, Leo points out that while he had left the game Street Fighter II, he used for his nickname "ZAN Ani (Elder Brother ZAN)," which actually referred to his beloved game character, ZANGIEF. Okamoto, for his part, explains that they, the developers, had to create a new game, because expert players were already using some tricks that they had found, also pointing out that they'd always consider including the characters that have pairs or affinity with each other.

This is all. I end here this part of the conversation between Okamoto-san and Leo.
