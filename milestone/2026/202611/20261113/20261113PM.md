In the video, Okamoto-san first admits that he talks in a way like the prior version of ChatGPT, so at times what he says might be untrue or a lie. He next talks about Jason Huang, a person who, he thinks, has a name very similar to Jensen Huang, the CEO of NVIDIA. He also reveals that there's now news circulating that Jensen Huang is having an affair with a spy from China. The woman, who's really 49 years old, but says that she's 42, in addition, is very well-educated and earns a lot of money in a high position. Jensen is reported to have shared with her information about the U.S. President Donald Trump, for example, who's told Jensen that he, the CEO of NVIDIA, has to sell their chips to China. Worse still, the woman has gone to Beijing, a very suspicious behavior, which is believed to have been done in order to avoid arrest.

NVIDIA, the giant company that Jensen Huang established when he was 30 years old after working as a dishwasher at 15, and graduating from Oregon State University before going to Stanford University, had begun by creating advanced chips in order to improve computer graphics. Despite many failures, it's now known that these semiconductors can also be used to improve AI. As a result, it's given Jensen an income 7,000 times more than what he had in the past.

However, the value of NVIDIA in the stock market has gone down sharply recently, and the company is in a precarious state, because people are asking if Jensen cannot take the helm, who then can fulfill the role? Okamoto-san says that NVIDIA's CFO seems to be the most suitable canditate for the position, although it's still doubted if the CFO could really assume the work of the CEO.

Finally, Okamoto-san responds to the questions of his viewers. For example, he says that, in his opinion, he thinks that the new Dragon Quest RPG by SQUARE ENIX for smartphones won't be a bestseller, because Yuji Horii, the creator of the franchise, isn't developing it. In addition, he points out that a Gacha or in-app purchase system doesn't blend well with RPGs. Moreover, the Dragon Quest franchise has been mainly one for the Japanese market, and every year that its players grow older, it becomes more and more challenging to sell the game as well.
