## --

Okamoto-san begins by saying that while many studios want to seize the opportunity to make a lot of money in the mobile gaming market, in reality, 80% of mobile gaming services already end in three years. Okamoto-san explains that this is because we must also take into account the expenses to maintain a mobile gaming service.

Okamoto-san points out that the studio has to pay for things like develop the game, announce it to the public, maintain the service every year in which a rather big title would need 100 million yen in its first year and millions of yen more for the additional payments thereafter. Thus, as a living thing, a studio, which doesn't make money and only spends, won't survive. Moreover, given that mobile gamers focus only on one game, not being the number one for these players means that a game that's only in the second or lower place won't last long. This is why it's now very common to hear news about studios going bankcrupt.

Of course, this situation isn't fun, particularly for the employees who'd need to accept a very low salary every month at only 100,000 yen and may have to listen to the excuse that, in exchange, they could do what they want.

Finally, since Okamoto-san pays money within the mobile games that he plays, if a studio would have to end a service of a game that Okamoto-san plays, and because of this, the things that he's accumulated in the game would also have to disappear, he'd also not like this outcome at all.
