First, Okamoto-san points out that this year, the world-renowned event celebrates its 30th anniversary and has received the highest number of visitors in its entire history.

However, Okamoto-san admits that while he regularly visited TGS in the past, he hasn't been going as much anymore this recent decade, because he explains that although the days for business people are, of course, reserved for them and aren't for the general public, the truth is that the studios don't use all the tickets that they can use, such that there's a market that has emerged, in which these tickets are sold, so that ordinary visitors could come to TGS during these days reserved for business people. Therefore, Okamoto-san recounts that this year, there was a line of visitors at the entrance so long that it seemed to have surpassed the one in Disneyland.

In addition, Okamoto-san says that when a visitor arrives at a studio's booth, he can't really try out their game, and would only exchange business cards with the presenter. Okamoto-san thus believes that it's better to come on the day before the event takes place and ask the manager to allow him to try out their game. Okamoto-san also mentions that during these recent years, he hasn't seen a truly revolutionary product, which means that, for Okamoto-san, it's not anymore economical to participate in TGS, given that he'll also have to pay for the hotel as well as the food when he and his team meet with the bosses of the studios with whom they'd want to work together.

In the end, while the visitors from China have now gone down due to the measures made by the Japanese government, Okamoto-san concludes that there are other, better activities to spend time than go to TGS.
