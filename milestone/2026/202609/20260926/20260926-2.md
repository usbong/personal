> I.T. Pro Stories: Anonymous Bank Deposits

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-09-26

## --

Dear friends,

Greetings of peace.

As most of you who've ever paid for something like school fees may know, you could do this by visiting your local bank and paying through a bank deposit, in which case you'd have to write down the school's account name, the amount and the bank's branch.

However, as it turns out, the school would not necessarily know who made the deposit without the payor notifying the school that he's already paid and has shown them the receipt. 

Because of this, there have been cases where people could receive a bank deposit without ever knowing who made the deposit. Moreover, the receiver of this deposit may not even know that there's been a deposit in the first place, unless he requests the bank to print him a bank statement with the history of the transactions involved with his account.

In local banks like the Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI), a depositor could be reminded by the bank teller to indicate the purpose of the payment, although this isn't strictly necessary. 

Thus, unless the account holder himself divulges his own personal information, the bank shouldn't be able to make public his personal details due to standard bank privacy laws. 

Of course, as in any country, we must use the internet while always keeping in mind that our private information could be sniffed, stolen and even modified by [criminals](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1144186) with stakes amounting to millions of pesos, even though there are already safeguards like https or VPN in place.

Having said this, I think that digital currencies that use Blockchain technology to clearly identify who is who and what is what were initially conceived to address this problem of anonymity, but ended up getting swayed away from its original purpose by people who wanted to avoid paying taxes, which, at the extreme, has led to the collapse of global cryptocurrency, FTX, back in 2022.
