> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: The Scriptwriter Noboru Sugimura (RIP)

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-09-23

## --

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture about scriptwriter Noboru Sugimura (RIP) with whom Okamoto-san worked during his time at Capcom as well as his own studio, Flagship.

Firstly, Okamoto-san admits that he doesn't anymore remember when he first met Sugimura-sensei, although he thinks that it was thanks to a colleague, who had introduced him to Sugimura-sensei, after which, they had gone out to drink Japanese sake together.
	
Okamoto-san next recounts that before meeting Sugimura-sensei, he was already well-known for writing scripts. Also, according to Okamoto-san, Sugimura-sensei enjoyed very much playing video games, giving an example of the first entry in the Resident Evil series, in which Sugimura-sensei discovered that a phrase appeared a bit odd, as though a third-person was speaking instead of the protagonist himself. The phrase said something like "It seems that the door cannot yet be opened right now." instead of "Agh! The door still won't open!"

Okamoto-san then asked Sugimura-sensei to write the script for the second entry of the series for 5 million yen, an amount that, Okamoto-san admits, wasn't a lot and without any additional incentives besides. Sugimura-sensei also worked in the first Resident Evil film with Okamoto-san, whom Sugimura-sensei allowed to change parts of the script. Afterward, Sugimura-sensei continued to work on Resident Evil: Code Veronica, although he told Okamoto-san that he'd like to form a team to help him as well as to train young colleagues.

Because of this, Sugimura-sensei invited Okamoto-san to establish a new studio to write scripts. Okamoto-san further reveals that, at the start, it was a studio 100% owned by Capcom called Flagship. In addition, this new studio would give them incentives depending on the performance of their projects. Okamoto-san gives examples of video game franchises that they worked on such as Fire Emblem and Zelda, also saying that Okamoto-san brought a development team to this studio.

However, while Okamoto-san continued working with Mr. Shinji Mikami and Mr. Keiji Inafune at Capcom as friendly rivals, he decided to leave Capcom in the end, such that Flagship also had to cut its relationship with Capcom. Shortly thereafter, Flagship went bankrupt.

In addition, when Okamoto-san was on a vacation in Thailand, he was informed that Sugimura-sensei didn't feel very well, and after drinking sake, he suddenly died while in his sleep. Because of this, Okamoto-san visited Sugimura-sensei's tombstone, and drank a Japanese sake in front, while remembering that a very talented person has passed away.

Finally, Okamoto-san advises us to comment about our recollections of the scripts during that era.

Therefore, I'd like to first admit that, during the PlayStation era, my English and Japanese were still not good enough to know subtle differences in the language like what Sugimura-sensei discovered; however, I can still recall that I was able to finish the Konami game, Vandal Hearts II, although I thought that the game's English script wasn't very easy to understand for me. 

When I finally played the English version of Final Fantasy Tactics the next summer or so, I was able to understand the basic plot without anymore a dictionary at hand and even reached up to the part after defeating the enemy and monster, Velius, though, in this case, I opted not to anymore read the side stories other than the main one. Curiously, the script of the latter seemed to be less difficult to understand than the one in Vandal Hearts II. 

Afterward, during the PlayStation 2 era, around the year 2002, I wasn't playing video games as much anymore, because I was focusing more on university where I met many friends. Perhaps, the experience was similar to the Famicom era, during which time I was playing traditional games with family members as well as the children from the shoe factory.
