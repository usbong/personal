First, Okamoto-san points out that, of course, above all, an aspiring producer must be a producer to have the opportunity to launch best-selling games. Therefore, how can this position be attained? Okamoto-san emphasizes that, in short, the company must have confidence in this aspiring person.

Okamoto-san next gives three main jobs, which a producer is the person most responsible for carrying out: one is attracting colleagues, second is bringing money to the team, and the last is creating the work environment.

Okamoto-san then explains that a work becomes a best seller when users affirm it. Okamoto-san also shares that to create video games is like raising a child, and, with the passage of time, the task becomes more fun, particularly because the creator also becomes better at doing it. Okamoto-san gives the example of running in which a child who runs 100 meters very quickly believes that running is very fun.

Next, Okamoto-san recounts that another reason why a producer doesn't achieve success is because he works as a subcontractor. Thus, he can't revise the budget that the main studio has given him when it subcontracted them. Moreover, this budget is related, in particular, to the deadline and available money. If the main studio no longer trusts the subcontractor where the producer works, it will be very difficult to change this budget.

As a result, the quality or user satisfication will also be low. Further, these things depend very much on the end product showing a sense of completion. While nowadays it's very easy to showcase works using AI, a long time ago, producers did it by speaking, writing essays or drawing illustrations.

Therefore, the ability to communicate well to the team prevents unnecessary changes in the direction toward the goal, something that affects the confidence of the team toward the producer. This is why Okamoto-san emphasizes that the producer must listen to his team, and that it's better that an idea that he has is the same one that his team is thinking about and that they tell him so as well, because the team's motivation rises in this way. This means that a producer who is capable of using this method instead of only ordering his team around has a much higher rate of achieving success. At this point, Okamoto-san points out that a mindset of not being miserable despite setbacks is very invaluable, so that a producer could achieve success.

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that launching works on time influences the outcome, such that it's easier to launch best-selling games while working in a big studio or one that really sells games and who can modify the schedule instead of only accepting it due to being only a subcontractor.
