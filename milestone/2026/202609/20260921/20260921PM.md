Okamoto-san begins by speaking about a Japanese family who had 2 billion yen of inheritance, but the eldest son chose not to get it, because he'd first have to pay 55% tax before being able to get the property, which still isn't in cash and he would have to sell.

Okamoto-san then explains that these inheritance taxes are too much, so much so that the Japanese, who can leave Japan, leave and now live in other countries, because they don't want to pay taxes for acquiring properties like this in Japan. Furthermore, Okamoto-san reveals that one of these countries that the Japanese prefer is Malaysia. In fact, for 14 straight years, Malaysia has continued to be the number one country for the Japanese who're thinking about living outside Japan.

Okamoto-san next compares the taxes in Japan with other countries, pointing out that at least about 50% of countries in the world don't anymore obligate citizens to pay inheritance taxes. Moreover, Okamoto-san gives as example China, where nobody's required to pay these taxes, and the U.S., where people only pay the amount over 200 million yen, while, in Japan, they must pay 30 million as well as 600,000 yen additional. Also, if there were three children who'd want to get the property, they must pay 600,000 yen additional each, in addition to the 30 million yen. Therefore, Okamoto-san advises lowering down these taxes to only 20% instead of 55%.

Okamoto-san complains that, to tell the truth, these taxes are only used by foreigners, especially those from China and even the newcomers, who could come to Japan and get welfare. He further points out that Japan still continues to donate money to countries like China, such that he thinks that the Japanese can't become rich, while more poor people continue to increase. Because of this, there are less Japanese who strive.

In the end, Okamoto-san concludes that during the war between Japan and Russia, the Japanese were made to pay taxes to help the country, but after the war, the taxes continued, and this situation continues to this day.
