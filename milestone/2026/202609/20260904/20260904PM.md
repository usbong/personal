> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: The Towerman Buildings in Central Tokyo

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-09-05

## --

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture about the Towerman ("Tower Mansion" or apartments that have 15 floors or more) in central Tokyo.

Okamoto-san tells us that given that the rise in the value of Towerman buildings has stopped, it's said that it will hence go down. Okamoto-san explains that this building seems perfect for "Power Couples" or pairs that don't have any child and both of the couples work, such that they have the money to buy an expensive unit with a very beautiful scenery in the evening.

Okamoto-san, however, reveals that when the unit is lit up, the truth is that this scenery can't be seen very well. Furthermore, Okamoto-san points out that, in reality, people buy a unit costing 100 million yen with the expectation that its value will go up to 160 million yen moving forward. But as the Japanese currency is currently going down, this value, if a couple, for instance, buys one now, won't go up so much later either.

Thus, it seems that the building was constructed more for rich foreigners; however, Japan's present administration is currently thinking about regularizing the purchase of housing units including land by foreigners. This is why less people want to buy housing units in Towerman right now. At this point, Okamoto-san gives the example of Singapore, where although Singaporeans pay more when they buy their second house, foreigners must pay 60% more when they buy their first house.

Okamoto-san next recounts that if a Towerman had been built in a place very close to the train station, the cost to build it would also be substantial. Moreover, although the maximum age that is allowed for a person to be able to borrow money from the bank is 80 years old, the apartments in Japan only last 50 years, which is why it's likewise asked what the value of buying a unit for people with high economic status is. 

Finally, Okamoto-san concludes that given these reasons, the value of Towerman buildings is foreseen to go down.
