Okamoto-san begins by asking Tanu-chan her impression about her salary when she joined Okamoto-san's team as a secretary. She answers that it was too much. Okamoto-san then points out that a student, who's paid a million yen to study in school, expects to receive 3 million each year as salary.

Okamoto-san also reveals that after employing a new graduate with good results, a studio would begin to want to employ more new graduates. However, with the passage of time, this studio would find itself in a situation wherein only 15% of its employees really strive, while the rest, not so much. With respect to this point, Okamoto-san gives the example of ants. He says that among these ants that pass by, there are only a few who actually carry the dragonflies (as food), pointing out that the world is like this as well.  

Okamoto-san also recounts that while he would never be able to enter the University of Tokyo in his lifetime, he's found that among the graduates of this prestigious university, there are fakes who don't have the high level of ability of the real ones. Okamoto-san explains that when students begin to look for work before graduating, those who really have a high level of ability also enter prestigious companies like Sony or Toyota, while the rest enter lower companies in terms of prestige.

Nonetheless, Okamoto-san clarifies that, of course, there are graduates who prefer to enter smaller companies for several reasons including learning more things by doing a multitude of tasks, unlike in a big company where graduates only focus on a few of their group or division's specialized tasks, though, as a tradeoff, it's harder to get a raise with respect to their salary in the latter. Furthermore, a graduate, who's learned a lot in a small company including the things that can't be learned in a big company, could move to a big company afterward, of course.

In the end, Okamoto-san concldues that the truth is that there are varieties of points of view to look at this topic, including the relationship between the sempai (senior employees) and the kohai (young employees), and he wants to tell us more about this subject in another video.
