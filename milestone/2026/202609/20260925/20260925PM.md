Okamoto-san asks Ms. Tapi and Ms. Tanu about their experience when they visit Starbucks, and they answer that the brand has the sense of selling high-class coffee, and thus are also quite expensive.

Okamoto-san agrees and admits that Starbucks' coffee isn't really as great as they make it out to be, recalling that Starbucks began by selling to countries such as the U.S. and Japan where people don't know very well which coffee is good. Also, with respect to this point, Okamoto-san gives the example of his young male colleagues, who order fruit drinks instead of coffee every time they go to Starbucks. Okamoto-san, for his part, prefers the americano, while Ms. Tapi drinks coffee like black coffee or the espresso with bagel.

Moreover, according to Okamoto-san, actually Starbucks sells something other than coffee, which is a third space after the house and the office. However, right now, in places like South Korea, people use this space too much, such that it's now prohibited to bring printers as well as partitions to Starbucks.

Further, Ms. Tapi shares that, in Malaysia, there's a drive-thru, in which the customer receives 5% discount. At this point, Okamoto-san points out that it seems that people don't take into account the cost of gasoline when they drive to Starbucks only to get this 5% discount, although it shouldn't be too bad if it's close to their main destination or is already on the way. Okamoto-san also thinks that it's better if a person can bring the receipt of their previous purchase to get, for example, 100 yen in their second visit, something that isn't done in Japan, but perhaps may be possible to do in foreign countries.

Finally, Okamoto-san reveals that he thinks that Starbucks needs to re-examine its business from the ground up instead of only creating more collaborations with other companies or franchises moving forward.

This is all. Of course, there's more to say, but I should already stop here. Thanks very much!
