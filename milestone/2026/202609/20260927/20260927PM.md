> Stories from Game Producer Yoshiki Okamoto: The issue that top-level managers in the video game industry don't play their own games

> Writer: [Michael B. Syson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelsyson/)

> Publication Date: 2026-09-28

## --

Dear friends,

Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's lecture about the issue that top-level managers in the video game industry don't play their own games.

## --

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gaE_iXTi63I; last accessed: 20260927

> 「ゲーム業界の経営層は自社ゲームを遊んでない」これほんと？

## --

Okamoto-san begins by talking about the remark by Mr. Katsuhiro Harada from VS Studio SNK, a new studio financed by the Saudi Arabian fund, that all the people at their studio really play their games. Okamoto-san, for his part, agrees in the value of this, pointing out that, in general, top-level managers in many studios don't really play as much, because acquiring the time to do it is very challenging, particularly if they also wanted to get enough sleep.

However, Okamoto-san recounts that, first, these managers should play the studio's games instead of only asking about the sales, the deadlines as well as the bugs, so he doesn't like hearing comments from people who've only played the game for only a short while. While he knows that immersing oneself into a game requires a lot of time, Okamoto-san admits that he and his team play until they throw up. Okamoto-san also invests money into the games that he plays more than people probably think, and he doesn't like it that a person would tell people to also invest if he himself doesn't do it. On this point, Okamoto-san emphasizes that he invests in mobile games, something that might be different from what Harada-san specializes.

Okamoto-san next admits that he doesn't also like hearing comments from a client who, although gives them money to create a game, doesn't actually play games. Okamoto-san then says that, of course, we want to hear that a person has played the game until the end, even if it's taken him a lot of time.

Finally, Okamoto-san advises us to share our thoughts about this topic as well.

Therefore, I'd like to say that two or more decades ago, when I was looking up how a newbie could enter the video game industry, I found some articles or blogs with words like "Megacorp," and which all still seemed to be from the U.S., wherein they wrote about the very common situation that well-known companies like Nintendo were facing, that is, they'd always get a lot of applicants who also had many ideas on how to improve the studio's games, and these studios, for their part, didn't like very much this type of applicant who was numerous. Thus, I think that gamers, on the other hand, may have played a lot, even completing the entire game, but it doesn't immediately mean that they equally understand how to create these games, nor run the business itself.
