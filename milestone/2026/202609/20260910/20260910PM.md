First, Okamoto-san reveals that he's asked his doctor to let him leave the hospital a day earlier after getting admitted to have the condition of his throat checked.

Next, Okamoto-san talks about the game, which was released in 1995 by Mr. Klaus Teuber in Germany during the time known as the Renaissance of Board Games in the beginning of 1990. According to Okamoto-san, Mr. Teuber was also known to be a dentist or a dental technologist. Okamoto-san also reveals that when Mr. Teuber suddenly died, Okamoto-san felt very sad, so much so that he lost his appetite to eat.

Okamoto-san next recounts that Mr. Teuber lived in the suburb, where he gradually bought houses near his first house within the neighborhood. Okamoto-san thinks that Mr. Teuber incorporated this very idea of acquiring properties in the gameplay of his game, Catan.

Okamoto-san also emphasizes that Catan has sold 50 million sets, a quantity that is only second in rank after Monopoly, another board game. Okamoto-san thus believes that Mr. Teuber was able to live very comfortably, given that each year the sales added up until the game had surpassed millions of sets sold around the world.

In addition, Okamoto-san points out that despite the pirated copies in the market, several notable varieties have been launched including Megaman Catan. Moreover, the game has been translated to 40 languages and is sold in 70 countries.

In the second part of the video, Okamoto-san further explains how to play Catan, pointing out the hexagonal panel that represents the recently discovered island called Catan, where each part signifies resources like lumber, metal and sheep, among others. Players have the goal of developing the island by creating roads, houses and more. Okamoto-san says that they must also look for the port in order to ship products.

Okamoto-san next points out that while up to four players can play it, he himself has created his own rule to make it more fun, and in this case, the game would only need three players, who must aim for the places on the panel that have numbers equal or very close to seven.

In addition to the hexagonal panel, Okamoto-san speaks about the two die that influence who among the players would win, explaining that when a player gets a combination of the numbers equal or very close to seven, something good or bad happens based on the rules, which, in general, favor getting a combination of numbers equal with seven.

This therefore means that the game changes every time the players play it, and the players who have houses in the places on the panel very advantageous to obtaining parts that have numbers very close to seven are also 60% more likely to win.
