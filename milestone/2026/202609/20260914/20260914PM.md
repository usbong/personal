Today let me share with you Mr. Yoshiki Okamoto's talk about the "potager," a French-style home garden, which he advises us to try to do as well.

To begin with, Okamoto-san thinks that the word, "potager," had probably come from another word, which is "potage," meaning vegetable soup.

Okamoto-san next admits that it's actually a hobby of his to cultivate a garden, which can be small or big depending on the person. Furthermore, Okamoto-san points out that it's very common that the owner takes by hand the vegetables in his own garden to be cooked or added in the food.

Okamoto-san also recounts that he's found that tomatoes, peas and eggplants are very easy to cultivate, although at times he's seen birds coming to eat the tomatoes even before he did.

Despite this, Okamoto-san wants us to be able to enjoy these four things from this experience: planning, cultivating, loving and eating what we plant. Okamoto-san also says that in addition to the vegetables, we can plant flowers as well as herbs if we wanted.

In the end, Okamoto-san advises us to experience this type of hobby as well.
